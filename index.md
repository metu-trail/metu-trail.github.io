## Why trustworthy and responsible AI?

AI systems now help decide who gets a loan, which medical scan gets a second look, and what
information millions of people see every day. When these systems are biased, opaque, brittle
or misaligned with human values, the harm is real and falls hardest on those least able to
contest it. Trustworthy and responsible AI (TRAI) is the effort to make sure AI is **fair** to
the people it affects, **protects their privacy**, can **explain** its decisions, stays
**robust** when conditions change or an adversary intervenes, and remains **aligned** with the
intentions and norms of the people it serves. These properties are not add-ons: they have to
be measured, designed and verified with the same rigour as accuracy. The Trustworthy and
Responsible AI Lab (TRAIL) at METU brings together four research groups working on these
questions, from theory to applications in computer vision, affective computing, multi-agent
systems and scientific machine learning.

## What we have contributed

- **Emre Akbaş** works on *concept-based explainability*: rethinking concept bottleneck
  models and fixing their pitfalls, explaining the zero-shot predictions of CLIP through
  human-understandable concepts, caption bottleneck models, and benchmarks for evaluating
  concept-based explanations. His group also studies out-of-distribution detection and
  data imbalance in face recognition.
- **Hüseyin Aydın** studies *privacy and cooperation in multi-agent and human-AI systems*:
  multi-agent reinforcement learning and computational theory of mind for resolving users'
  privacy conflicts, privacy dynamics in groups, fairness-aware incentives in sequential
  social dilemmas, and frameworks for human-AI interaction experiments.
- **Gökberk Cinbiş** works on *robustness and privacy of learning systems*: the adversarial
  robustness of zero-shot learning models, frequency-based data augmentation for model
  robustness (HybridAugment++), privacy-preserving collaborative learning, and reward-based
  tuning of generative image models.
- **Sinan Kalkan** works on *fairness and explainability*: bias and fairness in facial
  affect recognition and mental-health prediction (counterfactual and causal fairness,
  uncertainty as a fairness measure, fair multimodal self-supervised learning), quantitative
  and verbalised analysis of explanation heatmaps, explainable machine learning for
  scientific forecasting, disentangled representations, and adversarial robustness in object
  detection.
