Making AI trustworthy is not a single problem. A model can be accurate on average and still
be **unfair** to a demographic group whose data were scarce or skewed; it can be fair and
accurate yet leak the **private** information it was trained on; it can make the right
decision for reasons nobody can inspect, making it impossible to **explain** or to audit;
it can work in the lab and fail silently under distribution shift, noise or a deliberate
attack, i.e., lack **robustness**; and as systems become more autonomous, their goals and
behaviour can drift away from what people actually intend, the problem of **alignment**.

Each theme raises its own open challenges: how to measure bias when sensitive attributes are
missing and how to mitigate it without sacrificing utility; how to learn from, and reason
about, personal data without exposing it; how to produce explanations that are faithful,
human-understandable and quantitatively comparable; how to detect when a model should not be
trusted and train it to degrade gracefully; and how to encode human preferences, rewards and
social norms into learning agents. TRAIL studies these questions across computer vision,
affective computing, multi-agent systems and scientific machine learning. Our publications
on each theme are listed below.
